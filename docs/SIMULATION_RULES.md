## 0. The most important thing in this document

> ### ⚠️ ALL DATA IN SIMZONER IS MOCK / SYNTHETIC.
>
> SimZoner has **no real telemetry**. Not from Tesla, not from BMW, not from Waymo, not from
> TxDOT, IDOT, ODOT, or Houston METRO. Nothing in this system is fed by a live sensor, a live
> traffic API, or a fleet data feed. Every vehicle position, speed, traffic density, and race
> outcome this engine produces is a **synthetic number generated by our own physics code from
> hand-authored inputs.**
>
> Real-world highway facts are cited in this doc **only to make the mock world plausible.**
> A plausible mock is still a mock. Nothing SimZoner outputs is a prediction about how any real
> vehicle would perform on any real road, and it must never be presented as one.

Throughout this doc, every factual claim carries a tag:

| Tag | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **[VERIFIED]** | Checked against a cited public source. Real fact about the real world. |
| **[MOCK]** | Invented by us. Plausible, unmeasured, tuned for gameplay. Not real. |
| **[PUBLISHED]** | A manufacturer- or press-published vehicle spec. Real, but see §5.2 caveats. |
| **[OPEN]** | Unresolved. Needs an owner decision or more research. |

If you read only one section, read this one and §5.2 (spec honesty table).

## 1. Owner requirements as received, and how we interpreted them

The owner gave three directives. Two are clear. One is ambiguous and we are flagging it rather
than silently guessing.

| # | As stated | Our reading | Confidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | "3 HOV lanes for each of the cars" | **A single shared 3-lane HOV corridor** that all three vehicles compete in | ⚠️ **Ambiguous — needs owner confirmation** |
| 2 | "Obey speed limits, max 10 over" | Hard engine-level cap at `limit + 10` mph | ✅ Clear |
| 3 | "Race across US highways" | Segmented corridors, ~20 segments/race | ✅ Clear |

### 1.1 The HOV ambiguity — please read, owner

"3 HOV lanes for each of the cars" has at least three readings:

- **(A) A 3-lane HOV corridor shared by all three vehicles.** Nine lane-slots do not exist; there
  are three lanes, and the three vehicles contend for them. Overtaking, blocking, and lane
  choice all matter.
- **(B) Nine lanes total — three dedicated lanes per vehicle.** Each car gets its own private
  3-lane sub-corridor.
- **(C) "Three lanes" was shorthand for "a multi-lane highway with an HOV facility"** and the
  number 3 is incidental.

**We have proceeded on interpretation (A): one shared 3-lane HOV corridor.**

Reasoning, stated plainly so the owner can overrule it:

1. **(B) deletes the race.** If each vehicle has private lanes, the vehicles never interact.
   There is no overtaking, no blocking, no traffic to navigate around, no lane strategy. Three
   cars in three sealed tubes is not a race — it is three independent time trials that we could
   compute in closed form without a simulation. Given directive #2 caps speed (§3), removing
   interaction as well would leave literally nothing to decide.
2. **(A) is the only reading where the owner's own speed cap produces an interesting sim.** The
   cap makes strategy the deciding factor; strategy requires contention; contention requires
   shared lanes.
3. **No real US highway has 9 HOV lanes.** Real HOV facilities are typically 1–2 lanes per
   direction **[VERIFIED]** — TxDOT's I-35 Capital Express South project adds *two* HOV managed
   lanes per direction, not three ([TxDOT](https://www.txdot.gov/mymobility35/projects/capex-south.html)).
   A 3-lane HOV corridor is already at the generous edge of realism. Nine is fantasy.

> **[OPEN] OWNER DECISION REQUIRED:** Confirm (A), or correct us. If you meant (B), say so — it
> is buildable, it is just a different (and we think weaker) product, and we would want to
> re-scope before writing engine code. Everything in §2 assumes (A).

## 2. HOV lanes

### 2.1 What an HOV lane actually is **[VERIFIED]**

A High-Occupancy Vehicle lane is a lane restricted to vehicles carrying at least a minimum number
of people. The restriction is the entire point: it is a *demand-management* tool that trades lane
capacity for an incentive to carpool. The lane is fast **because** it is legally hard to enter.

Key real-world facts:

- **Federal floor: 2 occupants.** Under federal law the minimum occupancy a public authority may
  require is 2 people per vehicle ([AFDC / U.S. DOE](https://afdc.energy.gov/laws/HOV)). Agencies
  may set 3+; they may not set 1.
- **HOV-2+ vs HOV-3+.** HOV-2+ is the common default. HOV-3+ appears on the most congested
  corridors during peak windows.
- **HOV lanes are often time-restricted**, not 24/7.
- **Many HOV lanes are now HOT lanes** (High-Occupancy *Toll*): carpools ride free, solo drivers
  may buy in with a transponder. This converts a hard eligibility gate into a priced one.
- **Occupancy exemptions are narrowing.** California's Clean Air Vehicle decal program — which let
  solo EV/PHEV drivers use carpool lanes — **expired September 30, 2025**; from October 1, 2025
  solo EV drivers must meet standard posted occupancy rules ([LAist](https://laist.com/news/transportation/hybrid-ev-drivers-solo-carpool-lane-access-ends)).
  This matters to SimZoner: **"it's electric" is no longer an HOV pass.** The Cybertruck and the
  Waymo I-Pace get no occupancy exemption for being EVs.
- **Violation is enforced.** In Texas, occupancy/entry violations carry an administrative penalty
  of not more than $100 (Tex. Transp. Code § 452.0613(c)), and a contested citation in Justice of
  the Peace court can reach $500 ([FindLaw](https://codes.findlaw.com/tx/transportation-code/transp-sect-452-0613/)).

### 2.2 Do I-35 (Austin) and I-45 (Houston) really have HOV lanes?

Researched, because the answer is more interesting than a yes/no.

#### I-35, Austin — **HOV lanes are FUNDED AND UNDER CONSTRUCTION, NOT OPEN** **[VERIFIED]**

- TxDOT's **I-35 Capital Express South** project adds **two non-tolled HOV managed lanes in each
  direction** between SH 71 / Ben White Blvd and SH 45 SE
  ([TxDOT](https://www.txdot.gov/mymobility35/projects/capex-south.html)).
- These are **HOV, not toll lanes** — a deliberate TxDOT choice made over City of Austin lobbying
  for MoPac-style variable-toll express lanes
  ([Austin Monitor](https://austinmonitor.com/stories/2021/04/tolls-or-no-tolls-city-and-state-preferences-differ-for-new-i-35-lanes/)).
- **Austin's actually-operating managed lane is on MoPac (Loop 1), not I-35**: an 11-mile
  variable-toll Express Lane from Parmer Lane to Cesar Chavez, open since October 2017, free for
  transit buses and registered vanpools
  ([CTRMA](https://www.mobilityauthority.com/traveler-info/open-roads/mopac-express/)).

> **Honest statement for the doc:** as of this writing, **a driver on I-35 through Austin cannot
> use an HOV lane, because it isn't built yet.** SimZoner's I-35 HOV corridor is therefore a
> simulation of a *planned* facility. That is a legitimate thing to simulate — but we should label
> the route "I-35 (Capital Express, as-planned)" in the UI rather than implying it exists today.

#### I-45, Houston — **REAL, OPERATING, and the best model we have** **[VERIFIED]**

Houston METRO runs one of the oldest and largest HOV/HOT systems in the country
([METRO](https://www.ridemetro.org/riding-metro/hov-hot-express-lanes)):

- **Occupancy: generally HOV-2+** during operating hours (driver counts; children count).
- **US-290 escalates to HOV-3+** during the heaviest inbound window (6:30–8:00 a.m. weekdays).
  Same physical lane, different rule by time of day. This is the single most instructive real
  detail for our design — **eligibility is a function of (corridor, direction, time), not a
  vehicle property.**
- **HOT buy-in:** solo drivers may use the lanes by paying a toll with a valid tag (EZ TAG, TxTAG,
  and several regional tags).
- **Time-restricted and directional:** I-45 North inbound roughly 5:00–11:00 a.m., outbound
  roughly 1:00–8:00 p.m.
- **Critical geographic caveat for our Galveston route:** the I-45 South (Gulf Freeway) HOV/HOT
  lane runs **downtown ↔ Webster**. **It does not reach Galveston.** The HOV facility ends tens of
  miles short of the finish line.

> **Design consequence, and it's a good one:** on the real Houston→Galveston corridor the HOV lane
> *runs out mid-race*. Any vehicle that built its strategy around HOV eligibility gets dumped back
> into general-purpose traffic around Webster and has to re-merge. That is a free, real, dramatic
> strategic beat. **We should keep it rather than smoothing it away.**

### 2.3 The interesting problem: HOV eligibility for a driverless car

This is the sharpest rules question in the project, and it is genuinely unresolved **in the real
world**, not just in our sim.

**The setup:**

| Vehicle | Realistic occupancy | HOV-2+ eligible? |
|---|---|---|
| BMW M3 (owner-driven, racing) | 1 (the driver) | ❌ **No** |
| Tesla Cybertruck (owner-driven, racing) | 1 (the driver) | ❌ **No** |
| Waymo (autonomous, no rider) | **0** | ❓ **Legally undefined** |

Two observations that make this worth building around:

**(1) A racing car is the least HOV-eligible vehicle imaginable.** Nobody carpools to a race. If
we model occupancy honestly, *all three* of our vehicles are HOV-ineligible and the shared HOV
corridor from §1 sits empty. The premise "three racers in an HOV lane" is, taken literally, a
contradiction. We need a deliberate rule to resolve it — see options below.

**(2) The Waymo case is a real legal gap, not a puzzle we invented.** HOV statutes were written
assuming a human driver is occupant #1. A zero-occupant AV is arguably:

- **0 occupants** → ineligible, worse than the M3; or
- **1 occupant** (the Driver, in software) → still ineligible under 2+; or
- **∞ / exempt** (it's transit-like infrastructure, treat it as a bus) → always eligible.

Our research found **no statute or regulation resolving zero-occupant AV HOV eligibility**
([AFDC](https://afdc.energy.gov/laws/HOV) documents the federal 2-occupant floor and the exemption
categories; none addresses driverless operation). Federal law does carve out single-occupant
exemptions for certain categories — which is precedent that *the occupancy rule is negotiable for
policy reasons*, and the EV decal expiry is precedent that *such carve-outs get revoked*. The AV
question sits in exactly that unlegislated space.

**We are not going to pretend there is a right answer. We're going to make it a configurable rule
and let the race explore it.**

#### Proposed model: occupancy is a vehicle attribute; eligibility is a computed gate

```
occupancy: number            // per-vehicle, mutable across segments
hov_eligible(vehicle, segment, time) -> boolean
```

Eligibility is **computed per (vehicle, segment, time)**, never stored as a static vehicle flag —
because the real US-290 3+ peak rule proves eligibility is time- and corridor-dependent.

```
hov_eligible(v, seg, t):
  if seg.hov_lanes == 0:              return false      // no facility (e.g. I-45 south of Webster)
  if !seg.hov_active_window(t):       return true       // HOV rules off-peak → open to all
  if v.is_autonomous:                 return AV_POLICY  // ← the open question, see below
  return v.occupancy >= seg.hov_min_occupancy
```

#### Options for `AV_POLICY` — owner picks one (or we ship all three as a toggle)

| Option | Rule | Argument for | Argument against | Race effect |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **A. Strict** | AV occupancy = 0 → never HOV-eligible | Textually faithful: zero humans is zero occupants | Perverse — a Waymo removes a car *and* a driver from the road and gets punished for it | Waymo locked out of HOV; must win on traffic navigation and never needing a fuel stop |
| **B. Transit-equivalent** | AV counts as exempt, like a bus/vanpool | Matches the *policy intent* of HOV (move people efficiently, reward not-driving-alone); MoPac already exempts transit and vanpools **[VERIFIED]** | Empty AVs deadheading in the fast lane is the induced-demand nightmare planners fear | Waymo gets a structural advantage; race becomes "can horsepower beat lane access?" — **the most interesting matchup** |
| **C. Occupancy-passthrough** | AV eligible iff it carries ≥ `hov_min_occupancy` **riders** | Most honest to the letter *and* spirit: the lane rewards moving people, and an empty AV moves none | Requires modeling riders, which our racing premise doesn't naturally have | Waymo eligible only in a "carry passengers while racing" variant |

**Our recommendation: default to (B), ship (A) and (C) as config.** Rationale: (B) creates the
strategic tension the sim exists to explore — a slower vehicle (I-Pace, 395 hp **[PUBLISHED]**)
with lane privileges racing a faster one (M3, 503 hp **[PUBLISHED]**) stuck in general traffic. If
lane access can beat 108 horsepower, the sim has demonstrated something. If we pick (A), the Waymo
is just a slow car and there's no question to answer.

> **[OPEN] OWNER DECISION:** pick the default `AV_POLICY`. This is a values call about what the
> sim is *arguing*, not a technical one, so it should be yours.

#### Resolving the "racers aren't eligible" contradiction

Options, in our order of preference:

1. **Race with occupancy.** Give each vehicle a configured occupancy (M3: 1 driver; Cybertruck: 1
   driver + configurable passengers; Waymo: 0 + `AV_POLICY`). Ineligible vehicles simply **cannot
   use the HOV lanes** and must race in the general-purpose lanes. HOV eligibility becomes a
   *scenario variable* the owner tunes per race. **This is the honest model and it makes occupancy
   a real strategic lever** — a Cybertruck that loads 2 passengers takes a mass penalty (§5.2) in
   exchange for lane access. That trade is a genuinely good game mechanic and it falls out of
   modeling the world correctly.
2. **HOT buy-in.** Mirror Houston's real HOT rule: an ineligible vehicle may enter the HOV lane by
   paying a toll, modeled as a time or points penalty. Real, cited, and preserves access without
   pretending the rule doesn't exist.
3. **Model it as a sanctioned closed-course event** where the HOV corridor is reserved for the
   race. Cleanest narrative, but it discards the eligibility rule entirely — and the eligibility
   rule is the most interesting thing here. **Not recommended.**

#### Violation penalty **[MOCK, grounded in [VERIFIED] law]**

If an ineligible vehicle occupies an HOV lane:

| Parameter | Value | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| Detection | Probabilistic per tick, `p_enforcement` per segment | **[MOCK]** |
| Penalty | +90 s time penalty (modeled traffic stop) | **[MOCK]** — chosen for gameplay |
| Real-world anchor | ≤ $100 administrative; up to $500 in JP court | **[VERIFIED]** (Tex. Transp. Code § 452.0613) |

The 90-second figure is invented. It is *not* a conversion of the $100 fine — we chose it because
it must be large enough to make cheating a real decision and small enough that it isn't
race-ending. Tune freely; it carries no factual weight.

## 3. Speed & safety rules

### 3.1 The hard cap

> **`v_max = min(vehicle_top_speed, segment.speed_limit + 10 mph)`**
>
> **This is enforced in the physics integrator, on every tick, for every vehicle. It is a clamp,
> not a policy. There is no code path that exceeds it — not for the LLM driver agent, not for
> overtaking, not for "just this once."**

Design rules that follow:

1. The cap is applied **after** every acceleration computation, before position integration.
2. **No agent can request a speed above the cap.** The action space is bounded at construction, so
   an over-cap request is unrepresentable rather than rejected. Invalid states should be
   impossible to express, not merely caught.
3. If a vehicle enters a segment above the new (lower) limit, it must decelerate to legal speed
   within a **[MOCK]** compliance distance (proposed: 200 m) at a realistic braking rate.
4. The cap is a **hard invariant**: a property test asserts `∀ tick, ∀ vehicle: v ≤ limit + 10`.
   A violation is an engine bug, not a race event.

### 3.2 Why the cap makes this a *better* simulation

This deserves a real argument, because "capped speed racing" sounds like a lesser product. It isn't.

**First, the empirical point: the cap binds for all three vehicles, everywhere, so top speed is
already irrelevant.** Consider the fastest legal road in America:

| Vehicle | Published top speed | Cap on TX-130 (85+10 = **95 mph**) | Cap on I-35 Austin (70+10 = **80 mph**) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Jaguar I-Pace (Waymo) | 124 mph (limited) **[PUBLISHED]** | **95** | **80** |
| Tesla Cybertruck | ~112 mph **[PUBLISHED]** | **95** | **80** |
| BMW M3 Competition | ~155–180 mph **[PUBLISHED]** | **95** | **80** |

**On the fastest legal road in the United States, all three vehicles are pinned to exactly the same
number.** The M3's 60+ mph of top-end advantage is worth precisely zero. This isn't a limitation we
imposed on an otherwise-interesting race — **an uncapped race would be a spec-sheet lookup**: sort
by top speed, print the answer, no simulation required. The cap is what creates the need for an
engine at all.

**Second: it relocates the decision from the spec sheet to the driver.** With `v_max` shared, the
race is decided by:

- **Lane choice** — HOV vs general-purpose vs which of the 3 HOV lanes
- **HOV eligibility** — the §2.3 problem, now load-bearing
- **Traffic navigation** — finding gaps at Buda; the Brent Spence merge
- **Acceleration out of congestion** — where the M3's 503 hp and the Cybertruck's 845 hp **[PUBLISHED]**
  finally matter, because 0→70 mph *does* differ even when 70+ is capped. **Power still counts —
  it just counts in the 0-to-cap band instead of the top end.** This is where mass and power make
  the vehicles feel different.
- **Energy strategy** — charging/fuel stops (§3.5)
- **Risk** — HOV violation gamble, following distance vs. draft

**Third: it's the only version where the LLM agent has a job.** If the answer is "floor it," there
is no policy to set. The cap creates a decision space — which is the thing SimZoner is actually
built to explore. **The cap isn't a constraint on the sim. It's the premise of the sim.**

**Fourth: it's defensible.** A public artifact that simulates 150 mph highway running is
advertising reckless driving. One that says "everyone obeys the law; strategy decides" is a
legitimate engineering demo. We can show this to anyone.

### 3.4 Proposed safety rules **[MOCK — all constants invented, tune freely]**

| Rule | Model | Constants **[MOCK]** |
|---|---|---|
| **Safe following distance** | 2-second rule, scaled by traffic and grade | `t_gap = 2.0 s`; violation → forced decel |
| **Minimum gap floor** | Absolute floor regardless of speed | `d_min = 15 m` |
| **No unsafe passing** | Pass requires a target-lane gap ≥ `1.5 × d_follow` for the full maneuver | `safety_factor = 1.5` |
| **Lane-change cooldown** | No lane change within N s of the last one — prevents the agent from weaving frame-by-frame and forces committed decisions | `cooldown = 4.0 s` |
| **Lane-change duration** | A change occupies both lanes for a finite time; not teleportation | `3.0 s` |
| **No shoulder use** | Shoulders are not lanes, even the Causeway's full-width ones **[VERIFIED]** | hard rule |
| **Merge yielding** | Through traffic has right of way at on-ramps | hard rule |
| **HOV entry/exit discipline** | Only at designated access points, mirroring real METRO entry rules **[VERIFIED]** | per-segment access list |

The cooldown deserves a note: it exists for **design** reasons, not realism. Without it, an agent
optimizes into a physically absurd high-frequency weave. The cooldown forces lane choice to be a
*strategic commitment*, which is what makes it a decision worth handing to an LLM.

## 4. Routes

Each route is an ordered array of `HighwaySegment`s. **Segments are the decision points** — the
LLM driver agent is invoked at each boundary and nowhere else (§6). Target: **~20 segments/race**,
which is both a natural corridor decomposition and a manageable LLM budget on Cloudflare free tier
(~20 calls/vehicle/race).

Segment boundaries are placed where the *rules* change: speed limit, lane count, HOV availability,
traffic regime. Not at uniform distances.

### 4.1 Route: I-35 South → Austin

| Property | Value | Tag |
|---|---|---|
| Corridor | San Antonio ↔ Austin, ~100 mi, "busiest inter-metro Interstate in Texas" | **[VERIFIED]** ([Texas Highway Man](https://www.texashighwayman.com/i35corr.shtml)) |
| Traffic | >100,000 vehicles/day; ~5M-person megaregion | **[VERIFIED]** |
| Speed limit | 70 mph, Loop 1604 → SH 45 | **[VERIFIED]** |
| HOV | **Not built.** 2 HOV managed lanes/direction under construction, SH 71/Ben White → SH 45 SE | **[VERIFIED]** ([TxDOT](https://www.txdot.gov/mymobility35/projects/capex-south.html)) |
| ITS | Cameras, speed detectors, dynamic message signs since 2008 | **[VERIFIED]** |
| Notable | **Buda/San Marcos congestion** — the corridor's signature bottleneck | **[VERIFIED]** |
| Alternate | **TX-130 bypass, 85 mph**, built explicitly to pull traffic off I-35 | **[VERIFIED]** ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Texas_State_Highway_130)) |

**The TX-130 decision is the best single strategic moment in the project.** It's real: TX-130
exists specifically as an I-35 bypass and carries the highest posted limit in America. The agent
faces a genuine trade — longer distance at a 95 mph cap and a toll, vs. shorter distance at an
80 mph cap through the Buda grind. The answer depends on `traffic_density`, which the agent
observes at the segment boundary. **This is exactly the kind of decision the speed cap creates and
that an uncapped sim would erase.** Recommend making TX-130 a branch, not a separate route.

Traffic densities at Buda/San Marcos: **[MOCK]**. The congestion is real and verified; our numbers
for it are invented.

> **UI honesty note:** label this route **"I-35 (Capital Express, as-planned)"**. Its HOV lanes do
> not exist today.

## 6. Determinism

### 6.1 The contract

> **`same seed + same config + same engine version ⇒ bit-identical RaceResult`**
>
> Always. No exceptions. No wall-clock, no `Math.random()`, no `Date.now()`, no unordered
> iteration, no floating-point nondeterminism across the tick loop.

Requirements:

1. **Seeded PRNG only.** One explicit PRNG instance (e.g. a small xorshift/PCG), seeded from
   `RaceConfig.seed`, threaded through the sim state. **`Math.random()` is banned** — enforce with
   a lint rule, not a code-review convention.
2. **Fixed timestep.** `tick_hz` fixed (proposed 10 Hz). Never a variable delta from a frame
   clock. Physics integration must not depend on how fast the machine runs.
3. **No wall-clock in the tick loop.** Sim time only. `Date.now()` is banned in engine code.
4. **Deterministic ordering.** Vehicles processed in a stable, defined order. No `Set`/`Map`
   iteration where order affects results.
5. **No parallelism inside a tick** — or if introduced, only with a deterministic reduce.
6. **Version the engine.** Determinism is a promise *within* a version. Any physics change is a
   version bump; old results are re-runnable only at their recorded version. `RaceResult` records
   `engineVersion` for exactly this reason.
7. **Float discipline.** Same operation order every run. On Cloudflare Workers (single V8 runtime)
   this is tractable; note that cross-platform float reproducibility is a stronger claim we are
   *not* making. **We promise determinism on the Workers runtime, not universal bit-portability.**

### 6.2 Why this matters

- **Testability.** A test that asserts "seed 42 on I-45 finishes in 4,231.7 s" is a real
  regression test. Without determinism, every physics test is a flaky tolerance check and the
  suite rots.
- **Replay.** `RaceResult` stores a seed, not a video. A whole race is ~12 bytes of state plus the
  config — replayable, shareable, diffable. **On Cloudflare free tier this is the difference
  between storing seeds and storing gigabytes of telemetry.** Determinism is a hosting strategy,
  not just a code virtue.
- **Debugging.** "The Waymo clipped a barrier at tick 8,412" is only actionable if you can
  reproduce tick 8,412 on demand. Non-deterministic sims produce bugs that cannot be caught, only
  described.
- **Fairness / credibility.** If the same inputs give different winners, the sim is measuring
  noise. Determinism is what lets us claim a result *means* something within the model.
- **A/B rule comparison.** The entire §2.3 experiment — "does HOV access beat horsepower?" —
  requires running the same seed under `av_hov_policy: 'strict'` vs `'transit_equivalent'` and
  attributing the delta to the rule. **Non-determinism doesn't make that experiment harder; it
  makes it impossible.** Determinism is the precondition for the project's central question.

### 6.3 The LLM stays OUT of the tick loop

> **Hard architectural rule: the LLM sets policy at segment boundaries. The physics engine
> executes. The LLM is never called from inside the tick loop.**

```
  ┌─────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
  │  SEGMENT BOUNDARY  (~20 per race)               │
  │  → LLM driver agent invoked                     │
  │  → observes: traffic, HOV eligibility, energy,  │
  │    rivals, upcoming limits                      │
  │  → returns: DriverPolicy (a plain data object)  │
  └─────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
                       │
                       ▼
  ┌─────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
  │  TICK LOOP  (10 Hz, pure, deterministic)        │
  │  → executes the policy                          │
  │  → NO network. NO LLM. NO randomness but PRNG.  │
  │  → runs until the next segment boundary         │
  └─────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

```ts
interface DriverPolicy {
  targetLane: number;
  targetSpeedFraction: number;   // 0..1 of cap. CANNOT exceed cap — unrepresentable. (§3.1)
  useHovLane: boolean;
  acceptHovViolationRisk: boolean;
  payHotToll: boolean;
  followingAggressiveness: number;  // 0..1, scales t_gap within safe bounds
  plannedEnergyStop: boolean;
  takeBranch?: string;           // e.g. 'tx130' vs 'i35' (§4.1)
  reasoning: string;             // audit trail; does not affect physics
}
```

Why this boundary is non-negotiable:

1. **LLMs are non-deterministic.** Even at `temperature: 0`, output can vary across
   batching/hardware/model revisions. An LLM in the tick loop destroys §6.1 outright. **This alone
   settles the argument.**
2. **Latency.** 10 Hz × 20 min × 3 vehicles = ~36,000 inferences per race. At any latency, on any
   tier, this is not a system.
3. **Cost.** ~60 calls/race is a Cloudflare-free-tier product. 36,000 is not.
4. **Replay integrity.** Recorded `DriverPolicy` objects replay exactly. Recorded *prompts* do not
   — the model may not even exist next year.
5. **Separation of concerns.** Physics is physics; strategy is strategy. Mixing them means you can
   never tell whether a weird result is a bug or a bad decision. **Keeping them apart is what makes
   the sim debuggable at all.**

**The clean statement of the architecture:**

> **The LLM decides. The engine computes. The seed reproduces.**

If a race must be re-run to check an LLM decision, replay the recorded `DriverPolicy` array —
`(seed, config, policies) ⇒ identical result`, with no model call at all. **The LLM is a
compile-time input to the race, not a runtime dependency.**
